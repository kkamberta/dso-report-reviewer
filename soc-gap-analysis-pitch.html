1. The problem picture

Alerts interrupt the business before the story is clear.

SIEM, EDR, network, and cloud already generate alerts. The business pain is escalation quality: owners are interrupted before the team knows whether the event is real, important, and actionable.

Native SOC alerting today four consoles, one overwhelmed analyst
2. The fix

Each pain gets a process, not just a promise.

No new alerting tools. A review step sits after the four native sources and closes each gap above.

Pain
Process that closes it
The analyst sees a signal, not a complete incident story
An enrichment step attaches asset criticality, identity context, and threat intel to every alert before a human looks at it.
Context-switching across alert streams slows confirmation
A single intake step correlates alerts from SIEM, EDR, network, and cloud into one incident view instead of four separate consoles.
Low-value alerts compete with real incidents
A scoring step ranks alerts by combined signal strength and asset importance, producing a triage priority distinct from the raw alert severity.
Escalation happens before the next action is clear
Every escalated alert is checked for complete context, a triage priority, and a recommended next action before it reaches the analyst.
